The problem

Built apart. Wired together later.

Radar, electronic warfare, satellite links and telecom networks all pair high-frequency RF electronics with ever more digital processing. The two need different materials, fabrication and thermal conditions, so today they are made separately and joined through packages, interposers and boards.

The technology

Two sides. One sapphire wafer.

  1. Three layers, one platform

    RF on top, sapphire at the core, electronics below. Keep scrolling to take the wafer apart.

  2. The top face: power at frequency

    Gallium-nitride-family devices for high-power, high-frequency work: the RF front ends of radar, electronic warfare and communications systems.

  3. The core: one crystal, two jobs

    Single-crystal sapphire is an electrical insulator by nature. It gives both device layers a common foundation while keeping them electrically separate, and it is an established substrate for III-nitride growth.

  4. The bottom face: processing

    Single-crystal silicon-germanium for high-performance electronic and processing functions, grown on the opposite face of the same wafer.

  5. The paths between

    Inter-side signaling, isolation, thermal paths and RF and compute coexistence, designed and validated together as one architecture. The integrated device is under development.

Why Titanym

Integration at the wafer.

Heterogeneous systems usually meet at the package. Titanym moves the meeting point down to the wafer itself.

RF and compute, closer

Front end and processor normally sit on separate dies, packages and often boards. A shared wafer lets suitable designs place selected processing right behind the RF electronics.

Isolation in the material

Sapphire insulates the two device layers by nature, rather than by added packaging, and it is a substrate the III-nitride industry already knows well.

Interfaces by design

The signal, power and control paths between the two faces are designed and validated as part of the architecture, not bolted on afterwards.

Heat and reliability first

Power RF runs hot and precision electronics do not like heat. Thermal flow and long-term reliability are treated as primary design constraints from the start.

A proven materials base

Single-crystal silicon-germanium has been grown directly on sapphire at 450–500 °C in published research and characterized by X-ray diffraction, AFM and TEM. Titanym builds from that foundation; the integrated RF and compute platform is under development.

Not a replacement for every process. A new integration path where conventional approaches force costly tradeoffs.
